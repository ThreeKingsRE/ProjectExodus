## Riverknoll Renewal: rebuilding RIT's most affordable housing

RIT is closing Riverknoll after the 2026–27 year. This initiative asks what it would take to rebuild it as affordable, all-electric townhomes, and shows the work so anyone can check it.

### What we propose

- **744 new beds:** sized to RIT's own enrollment and housing data, not to a drawing. The sourced range is 444 to 871.
- **18 rows of 10 townhomes:** one repeated design, private bedrooms, 72 accessible ground-floor beds.
- **Built in phases:** design and permits in year 1, a 72-bed pilot in fall 2029, then 4 rows a year to 2033.
- **About $168M, or $225k a bed:** assuming RIT's decommissioning clears the old buildings (our estimate of that is about $22.6M, shown separately).

### Who could pay for it

Today's Riverknoll rent can repay about a quarter of the cost, whoever borrows. A nonprofit owner or developer on an RIT ground lease changes who signs the loan, not the size of the gap.

- **At Riverknoll-level rent:** RIT would carry about $76M to $117M.
- **At off-campus-level rent, with smaller homes:** RIT's share falls to about $13M to $53M, but students would pay about 1.5 times today's rate.

Grants and gifts we found add about $10.7M in the base case and up to $38.6M at best. Low-income housing tax credits and HUD college loans do not apply to student housing.

### Every option, side by side

We costed 32 titled options across design, phasing, cost cases and funding, each with its cost per bed, RIT's share, risks and sources. Read them in the options comparison and the research library, and tell us which approach you would choose.
