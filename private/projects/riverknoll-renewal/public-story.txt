[Take the Riverknoll housing survey](https://forms.gle/ZZmocqQbpuf9BHMW8)

Riverknoll Renewal is a student-led proposal for affordable upperclass housing at RIT. RIT plans to close Riverknoll after the 2026–27 year. This page asks what it would take to rebuild it as affordable, all-electric townhomes, and shows all of our work, every version and every option, so anyone can check it and choose for themselves.

The initiative is independent. RIT has not approved or funded it. Figures are concept research and allowances, not bids. Updated November 26, 2026 (model v24.1 with its wood-frame comparison plan, material list v2, System 3E vs wood frame life cycle v2, deck v26).

## Latest version: model v24.1, System 3E and wood frame side by side

The newest work comes first; every earlier version stays on this page below. v24.1 keeps the whole building in System 3E and assumes crews stack the blocks as fast as System 3E says (145 square meters of wall in 1 day instead of 14). The same model built in ordinary wood framing stays here as a second plan, so you can compare the two and choose.

| What | System 3E plan (base) | Wood-frame plan (comparison) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walls and floors | Dry-stacked perlite blocks for outside walls, walls between homes and partitions; concrete plank floors | 2x6 wood framing with insulation, wood party walls and partitions, wood floor joists, advanced framing |
| Total | $173.7M | $167.6M |
| Per bed | $233,442 | $225,240 |
| Per sq ft, buildings only | About $294 | About $284 |
| Per sq ft, all in | About $575 | About $555 |
| RIT's share (base funding) | About $123.2M | About $117.1M |
| Wall labor | About 49,800 hours, mostly trained general labor | About 62,900 hours of framing carpentry |
| Schedule | About 1.3 weeks faster per phase; homes still open each fall | Base calendar |
| Upkeep over the years | About $133 a bed a year less (insurance, refinishing, wall holes, energy, hidden wet walls) | Base |

Does System 3E pay for itself over time? The walls alone cost about $2,600 a bed more up front and pay that back in about year 28 (worth about $1,300 a bed over 50 years at 5.15%). The whole System 3E building with concrete floors costs about $7,800 a bed more and doesn't pay back within 50 years on upkeep alone; the concrete floors buy fire separation, quiet and a wood-free building. More than half the yearly saving is insurance, so an RIT insurance quote by building type matters, and so does a real System 3E price: each $1 a sq ft of wall moves the answer about $660 a bed. If the first US crews stack no faster than ordinary block, System 3E walls run about $13,800 a bed behind.

Material list v2 counts every material the design needs, nothing more and nothing less: about $162 a sq ft to build and $320 all in, $130,049 a bed, from national installed list prices. The model keeps the more cautious $294 until a Rochester contractor prices it.

## Model v24: why the whole building is System 3E

| What changed in v24 | Result |
| --- | --- |
| The whole building is System 3E: dry-stacked perlite blocks from Poland for outside walls, walls between homes and partitions, with concrete plank floors | $177.0M, or $237,854 a bed (v23 was $158.4M, $212,839 a bed) |
| Cost per square foot | About $305 a sq ft for the buildings alone, about $595 a sq ft all in (design, contingency, inflation, financing) |
| Were walls counted twice? | No. Walls were counted once, as wood framing inside the building price. v24 swaps each wood part for its System 3E part, about $9.4M more than wood framing, and drops the earlier, wrong 5% panel saving |
| How sure the System 3E price is | US prices we built up part by part, not a System 3E quote: $221k to $267k a bed between the low and high cases |
| Floors | Concrete plank in the base. Wood joists would be $232,679 a bed; floors poured on site are an option we have not priced yet |
| Insulation | On the maker's data the block wall meets the NY energy code with no added insulation. More insulation doesn't pay back; air sealing does, in about 14 years |
| Fire | The wall lasted 244 minutes in a European fire test, well past the 1 to 2 hours a townhouse wall needs, but a code official must accept the European test or ask for a US one |
| Local materials | Everything but the System 3E blocks comes from suppliers within a couple of miles of the site, listed in material list v1 |

## What we propose now

| Item | Current plan |
| --- | --- |
| New beds | 744, sized from RIT's own enrollment and housing data (sourced range 444 to 871) |
| Homes | 18 identical rows of 10 townhomes, private bedrooms, one repeated design, built in System 3E perlite blocks |
| Accessible beds | 72 single-level ground-floor beds, against about 38 required |
| Phasing | Design and permits first, a 72-bed pilot in fall 2029, then 4 rows a year, last homes 2033 |
| Total cost | About $174M, or $233k a bed (System 3E stacked at the maker's speed); about $168M in wood frame |
| Old buildings | Assumed cleared by RIT's own decommissioning; our estimate of that is about $22.6M, shown separately and not in the total |

The bed count comes first and the design meets it. 744 beds would house about 53% of RIT undergraduates on campus, against a peer median of about 60%.

## Who could pay for it

Today's Riverknoll rent can repay about a fifth to a quarter of the cost, whoever borrows. A nonprofit owner or private developer on an RIT ground lease changes who signs the loan, not the size of the gap.

| Scenario | RIT's share |
| --- | --- |
| RIT borrows (base), Riverknoll-level rent | About $123M |
| Other owners and loan terms, same rent | About $112M to $133M |
| Smaller peer-sized homes | About $95M |
| Plus rent near off-campus levels (about 1.5 times today) | About $60M |
| Plus every gift and grant at its high end | About $32M |

Grants and gifts we found add about $10.7M in the base case and up to $38.6M at best. Low-income housing tax credits and HUD college loans do not apply to student housing. The trade-off is clear: the less RIT pays, the more students pay or the smaller the homes.
