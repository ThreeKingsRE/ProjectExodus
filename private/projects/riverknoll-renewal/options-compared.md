# Riverknoll Renewal: every option, side by side

Draft for review, November 26 2026 (model v24, the latest version: the whole building in System 3E perlite blocks). Not an approved RIT project. Every option below keeps the same demand-led program of 744 new beds, so the numbers compare like with like. Earlier versions of this comparison live in models v21.1 to v23, all still on this page.

How to read it:

- **Total:** total project uses, including design, permits, contingency, inflation to each phase and financing.
- **Per bed:** total divided by 744 new beds.
- **RIT net share:** what is left for RIT after the debt that rent can carry and the base outside money ($10.7M of grants and gifts). RIT's own decommissioning of old Riverknoll (our estimate about $22.6M all in) is not in these totals: the plan assumes RIT carries it as the site's owner, since Riverknoll closes after the 2026–27 year either way. RIT has not published a demolition plan. Options C6 and C7 show the totals if this project paid it instead.
- **Status:** *Adopted* means it is in the current base plan; *Option* means it is costed but not chosen.

Source workbook: Riverknoll feasibility model v24, Options_Compared tab, where each line points to the cells and sources behind it.

## What it adds up to

Design and cost choices move the total by tens of millions of dollars; who borrows moves RIT's share by a few. v24 builds the whole building in System 3E perlite blocks (D6): exterior walls, walls between townhomes and partitions, with precast concrete plank floors. Priced part by part against the wood framing it replaces, that costs about $9.4M more than conventional framing (D7), about 4% of the building. Those are US prices we built up, not a System 3E quote; with every part at its low or high end the total runs from about $164.5M to $198.8M. Floors poured on site are an option not yet priced, and wood floor joists instead of plank would bring the total to about $173.1M ($232,679 a bed).

The biggest single lever on RIT's share is rent. At today's Riverknoll rent, grown 3% a year, RIT carries roughly two-thirds or more of the cost under every ownership structure. RIT's share gets near $62M only by combining peer-sized homes and rent near what students pay off campus today (about 1.5 times Riverknoll's rate), and about $35M if every gift and grant also lands at its high end. That trades away some of the affordability the plan is for, so the plan shows both: about $98M to $126M at Riverknoll-level rent, or about $35M to $91M at off-campus-level rent.
