# Riverknoll Renewal: every option, side by side

Draft for review, November 26 2026 (model v22, the latest version). Not an approved RIT project. Every option below keeps the same demand-led program of 744 new beds, so the numbers compare like with like.

How to read it:

- **Total:** total project uses, including design, permits, contingency, inflation to each phase and financing.
- **Per bed:** total divided by 744 new beds.
- **RIT net share:** what is left for RIT after the debt that rent can carry and the base outside money ($10.7M of grants and gifts). RIT's own decommissioning of old Riverknoll (our estimate about $22.6M all in) is not in these totals: the plan assumes RIT carries it as the site's owner, since Riverknoll closes after the 2026–27 year either way. RIT has not published a demolition plan. Options C6 and C7 show the totals if this project paid it instead.
- **Status:** *Adopted* means it is in the current base plan; *Option* means it is costed but not chosen.

Source workbook: Riverknoll feasibility model v22, Options_Compared tab, where each line points to the cells and sources behind it.

## What it adds up to

Design and cost choices move the total by tens of millions of dollars; who borrows moves RIT's share by a few. v22 adopts System 3E prebuilt facade and floor panels (D6), which saves about $8.3M on the v21.1 base; that 5% saving comes from general panel research, not a System 3E quote, so it could be anywhere from nothing to about 10% until System 3E bids. The biggest single lever on RIT's share is rent. At today's Riverknoll rent, grown 3% a year, RIT carries roughly two-thirds or more of the cost under every ownership structure. RIT's share gets under $50M only by combining peer-sized homes and rent near what students pay off campus today (about 1.5 times Riverknoll's rate). That trades away some of the affordability the plan is for, so the plan shows both: about $73M to $109M at Riverknoll-level rent, or about $20M to $60M at off-campus-level rent.
