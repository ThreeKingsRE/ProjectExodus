# Riverknoll Renewal research library

Draft for review, November 25 2026. Concept research by the initiative organizer and collaborators. Not an approved RIT project, bid or permit set. Every figure is an estimate from the feasibility model unless it says otherwise, and each section names where to check it.

Current working files (in the organizer's Drive, pending review before they replace the hosted review set): feasibility model v21.1 and pitch deck v24. The hosted review set on this site is still workbook v12 and deck v19.

## 1. What happens to Riverknoll

- RIT has announced that Riverknoll closes permanently after the 2026–27 academic year. No demolition or replacement plan was stated (RIT Reporter, Nov 9 2026: https://reporter.rit.edu/8904/showcase/riverknoll-to-be-permanently-closed-after-2027-academic-year/).
- RIT's 2022 campus master plan anticipated a new residence hall that would allow Riverknoll to be retired (RIT News, Dec 5 2022: https://www.rit.edu/news/rit-readies-campus-master-plan-will-guide-future-growth-and-development).
- RIT's October 2026 capital budget has no Riverknoll line; October 2024 listed $990,000 of foundation repairs (https://www.rit.edu/fa/sites/rit.edu.fa/files/October%202026%20Capital%20Listing.pdf).
- Built 1970 as modular wood-frame apartments and townhouses on about 14.5 acres with 328 parking spaces (DASNY/RIT 2019 SEQR: https://www.dasny.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/RIT%202019%20Financing%20Type%20I%2009.10.19%20SES.pdf).

Working assumption: RIT's decommissioning pays for demolition and asbestos abatement. Our estimate of that cost is kept visible, outside this budget: about $12.7M at today's prices, about $22.6M all in (model, Value_Engineering Parts G and H).

## 2. How many beds

The plan is demand-led at 744 new beds, with a sourced range of 444 to 871 from RIT's Common Data Sets and housing figures (model, Demand tab). The historic 513-bed count and the 444 beds in RIT's 2022 Campus Plan still need confirming against RIT's current inventory.

## 3. Design

- 18 identical townhome rows of 10 units, 12 two-story and 6 three-story; a compact 25 x 32 ft four-bed plan (400 GSF a bed); 72 accessible ground-floor beds against 38 required.
- Townhouse rules (IRC R202 and R302.2): side by side, foundation to roof, open on two sides, at most 3 stories; no plumbing in party walls.
- Fire access: a road within 150 ft of every first-floor wall, so a 250 ft row works with a lane on one side; longer rows need lanes on both sides or an approved extension (confirm in the 2025 NY Fire Code).
- New townhouses on public water need sprinklers, and new low-rise buildings must be all-electric in New York (NY All-Electric Buildings Act).

## 4. Cost

- Base: $167.6M total, $225k a new bed, after RIT's decommissioning takes demolition and asbestos off this budget.
- From v19 to v20.1: compact plan −$44.6M; reused roads and parking −$6.1M; one drawing set −$7.5M; advanced framing −$2.4M; pilot then 4 rows a year +$12.2M; RIT's decommissioning −$22.7M.
- Utilities: inspect and line old sewer mains, replace what fails; new laterals, water services and electric service are needed either way. Flow rises about 45% at 744 beds, so the sewer and electric capacity must be checked with RIT Facilities, MCWA, Henrietta Sewer District and RG&E.
- Asbestos: certified survey before demolition and licensed abatement under NY Industrial Code Rule 56 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/title-12/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-56; NYS DOL P231: https://dol.ny.gov/new-york-state-asbestos-survey-requirements-building-permit-applicants-p231).
- Salvage saves almost nothing once removal labor is counted; donating to the Habitat ReStore in Henrietta is an option.

## 5. Phasing

Year 1 is design, permits and surveys. A 2-row, 72-bed pilot opens August 2029, then 4 rows a year, last homes August 2033. Year 1 spend is about $6.0M and the largest year about $39.7M. Faster patterns cost less overall but need $53M to $71M in a single year (model, Phase_Plan).

## 6. Funding: who pays, and how little can RIT pay

Who borrows changes RIT's share only a little. Today's Riverknoll rent can repay about $30M to $51M of debt, depending on who borrows, against a $168M cost.

| Who owns and borrows | RIT net share | Share of cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RIT, DASNY bonds over 30 years (base) | $117.1M | 70% |
| RIT, DASNY bonds over 40 years | $111.7M | 67% |
| Nonprofit owner on an RIT ground lease | $123.4M | 74% |
| Same nonprofit, weaker loan terms | $126.6M | 76% |
| Private developer on a ground lease | $105.8M | 63% |

Outside money: about $10.7M in the base (NYSERDA heat-pump incentive and gifts), up to $38.6M if every source lands high.

- Can qualify: NYSERDA New Efficient Homes heat-pump incentive (https://www.nyserda.ny.gov/All-Programs/New-Efficient-Homes-New-York/Heat-Pump-Incentive); NYSERDA Buildings of Excellence, uncertain for student housing; the federal geothermal heat-pump credit as cash for a tax-exempt owner, only with ground-source heat pumps (IRS elective pay: https://www.irs.gov/node/123396); RG&E ground-source rebate (https://www.rge.com/documents/d/global/building-electrification-rebate-catalog-pdf); HECap, uncertain because residence halls are not named (https://dasny.org/sites/default/files/cta-link-files/2025/HECap%20FAQ.pdf); philanthropy, with RIT's naming policy reserving residence halls for commemorative naming (https://www.rit.edu/giving/sites/rit.edu.giving/files/RIT%20Gift%20Acceptance%20and%20Naming%20Policy_Revised%202025_final.pdf).
- Ruled out: low-income housing tax credits (full-time student rule, IRC 42(i)(3)(D), and the general public use rule, 26 CFR 1.42-9), HUD college housing loans, Empire State Development capital (excludes residential-only projects), NY-Sun upstate commercial, 45L and 179D (ended for construction after June 30 2026: https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/faqs-for-modification-of-sections-25c-25d-25e-30c-30d-45l-45w-and-179d-under-public-law-119-21-139-stat-72-july-4-2025-commonly-kno), and the 48E solar credit (must be in service by end of 2027).
- Precedents for a nonprofit or developer partner on campus land: Lynn University and Provident ($81.6M, 743 beds), URI and Gilbane (about 1,100 beds, $210M tax-exempt bonds, Nov 2025: https://www.uri.edu/news/2025/11/uri-gilbane-reach-financial-close-on-partnership-to-build-and-operate-new-on-campus-student-housing/). Rating agencies count such debt with the university (Moody's higher education methodology), and partner housing debt has been downgraded when occupancy fell (Texas A&M Park West).
- RIT's capacity (FY2026 audited statements: https://www.rit.edu/controller/sites/rit.edu.controller/files/2026-11/FY2026%20RIT%20Financial%20Statements%20Signed.pdf): endowment $1.683B, debt principal $377.7M, liquid assets within a year $464.8M. Borrowing the $128M gap would add roughly $7.9M a year of debt service, about 1% of revenue (our estimate).

What it takes for RIT to pay a minority share:

| Combination | Total cost | RIT net share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Base plan, Riverknoll-level rent | $167.6M | $117.1M |
| B. A with peer-sized homes and wall panels | $126.9M | $76.4M |
| C. A with rent near off-campus (about 1.5x Riverknoll) | $167.6M | $81.4M |
| D. B and C together | $126.9M | $40.8M |
| E. D with every gift and grant landing high | $126.9M | $12.9M |
| F. D through a nonprofit owner | $126.9M | $52.8M |

To cap RIT at $50M on the base plan, rent would need about $13,200 a bed-year in today's dollars, nearly twice Riverknoll's $6,762 and above APEX's about $10,188. Phasing helps cash flow, not the total: old Riverknoll closes after spring 2027, and each new phase's rent starts repaying its own loan.

The realistic route for the initiative: Projxodus as initiator and advocate; an RIT call for proposals that invites established nonprofit owners and developers; and a student and community advisory seat written into any ground lease. A student or a new nonprofit cannot realistically own or borrow for the project.

## 7. Wider value

- Long run: on rent alone the rebuild does not pay for itself at RIT's borrowing cost; it is just above even once wider benefits are counted (model, Long_Run and Co_Benefits).
- Market: with no rebuild, about 340 more students would rent in Henrietta, pushing local rents up slightly (model, Market_Impact).
