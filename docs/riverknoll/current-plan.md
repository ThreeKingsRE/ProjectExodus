# Riverknoll Renewal

Revision: 2026-11-12.1 · November 12, 2026

Initiated by Riverknoll Student

**Proposal in development.** Student-originated proposal; not approved or funded by RIT.

A student-led proposal for affordable upperclass housing at RIT, connecting thoughtful homes, a practical construction transition, shared gardens, and responsible use of food, water, and energy.

## Purpose and Exodology

Bring residents, designers, builders, and the RIT community together to develop an evidence-based housing proposal. Compare System 3E and conventional construction, protect affordability and existing neighbors, and make the design, costs, and decisions open to review.

Riverknoll Renewal is the creator’s first Exodus design: a developing application of Exodology, the discipline Mr. Nobody is working to establish. Housing, ecology, technology, institutional decisions, and community participation are studied together. This proposal will document its methods and decisions as it develops; outcomes remain to be tested.

## Design requirements

### Homes that work for students

Study private-bedroom rowhouses and a genuinely spacious shared-bedroom alternative. Keep shared kitchens and living rooms usable. Include an accessible ground-level option and evaluate the circulation and fire-safety implications of added stories.

### Keep the neighborhood connected

Preserve Greek Row on Charters Way, Riverknoll 516–536, mature trees where feasible, and resident and emergency access. Survey buildings, root zones, utilities, grades, and routes before fixing new footprints.

### A courtyard with a working water path

Trace roof runoff through gutters, screened inlets, sediment treatment, a planted basin, and a designed overflow. Study irrigation and winter safety. Keep salted pavement runoff separate unless treatment is validated; fish are an optional engineered study, not a promised feature.

### Build a community, then keep it usable

Connect small private café patios, accessible shared paths, gardens, and hardy fruit trees. Place habitat away from foundations and ventilation. Homes are unfurnished apart from fixed kitchen and bathroom fixtures and appliances.

## Delivery sequence

1. **Understand the site:** Verify current capacity, retained beds, conditions, trees, utilities, hazardous materials, access, and resident priorities.
2. **Compare and price:** Test the same housing design with System 3E and conventional assemblies. Obtain local code, supply, crew, cost, and whole-building energy review.
3. **Test a summer package:** Ask a construction manager to resource two-building and four-building packages, including utilities, temporary works, inspections, commissioning, and fall occupancy. Compare a full-closure alternative and swing housing.
4. **Finish each courtyard once:** Complete the buildings, underground work, and heavy delivery routes affecting that zone before permanent paths, pond, and planting. Open accepted replacement housing before releasing the next occupied zone, where safe and feasible.

## Financial screen

Working target: 513 total beds. Retained capacity: unverified; all target beds provisionally priced as new. New beds screened: 513. Gross building area: 179,550 SF.

Academic-year rent reference: $6,762 per bed; occupancy 95%; operating allowance 40%; debt rate 5%; amortization 30 years; debt coverage 1.20x. Modeled NOI: $1,977,276. Illustrative debt supported: $25,329,654. Base capital gap: $133,541,301.

Wall-only savings screen: $4,628 per year, using the earlier R-22 vs R-27.8 screen. 20-year present value: $67,871 at 5% discount and 2% escalation. No maintenance differential is assumed; System 3E premium remains unquoted. This is not a whole-building forecast or proof of payback.

2026-dollar concept allowances carried from feasibility workbook v11, not contractor bids. A blank retained count prices every target bed as new for screening only. Demolition, abatement, and temporary works require independent scope quantities; per-bed scaling is an early proxy. The rent reference is an academic-year room rate, not a twelve-month lease. Debt capacity uses the gross target’s rent, assumes retained revenue is available, excludes existing debt and retained-building renewal costs, and is therefore optimistic. DSCR 1.20 is a new illustrative lending constraint; workbook v11 did not apply it.

- Reconcile 513 historic beds with the 444-bed campus-plan figure and the current housing inventory.
- Verify how many beds remain at 516–536; retained beds count toward the gross target.
- Confirm System 3E’s project-specific New York code path, assemblies, supply, installer requirements, and installed price.
- Obtain Riverknoll-specific utility and maintenance records; campuswide budgets cannot stand in for them.
- Develop measured plans, an accessible circulation strategy, drainage engineering, and contractor-tested phasing.

## Participation

### What should future Riverknoll preserve?

Tell us what affordability, privacy, accessibility, kitchens, shared spaces, and campus proximity mean in daily life.

### What would make a summer phase credible?

Help identify crew sizes, procurement lead times, utility shutdowns, inspection gates, safe access, and temporary housing needs.

### What evidence would change the design?

Contribute current bed counts, local pricing, utility and maintenance records, building-system expertise, and stormwater review. Do not post private resident information.
